Legio III Gallica

This is a secondary Legion who attacked Jerusalem in 70AD.

Legio III Gallica (Third Legion "Gallic"): Stationed in Syria, elements of this legion provided additional veteran support to Titus's army.

Legio III Gallica (Third Gallic Legion) was not composed of a single nationality throughout its four-century existence; rather, its makeup changed significantly depending on the era and where it was stationed:

  • Initial Foundation (c. 49–48 BC): Raised by Julius Caesar for his civil war against Pompey, the legion was originally recruited from the inhabitants of Cisalpine Gaul (northern Italy) and Transalpine Gaul (southern France/Provence). This Gallic origin gave the unit its surname Gallica.
  • Early Imperial Period & Move to Syria (30 BC onwards): After Octavian (Augustus) became emperor, the legion was stationed in Syria. Over time, as original veterans retired, the unit relied heavily on local recruitment from the Eastern Mediterranean—primarily Syrians, Greeks, Galatians, and Anatolians who were granted or already held Roman citizenship.
  • Later Roman Period: As local regional recruitment became standard across the Roman Empire, the composition of the legion became almost entirely Syrian and Near Eastern, fully absorbing local cultures (such as adopting the Eastern sun-worship practices famously displayed during the Battle of Bedriacum in AD 69).
