What nationality was the make up of the Roman legions who destroyed the temple in 70 AD?

Four Roman legions—Legio X Fretensis, Legio V Macedonica, Legio XV Apollinaris, and Legio XII Fulminata—took part in the siege and destruction of the Second Temple in 70 AD, drawing their soldiers from provincial regions across the Roman Empire rather than the city of Rome itself.

Notice the prominent nationality of each legion is Syrian.

Legio X Fretensis

  • Original Foundation: Raised by Octavian (later Emperor Augustus) in 41/40 BC.
  • Provincial Origin / Makeup: Originally tied to Italy and Sicily, by 70 AD its ranks were heavily filled with Syrian and Eastern provincials. Quora Legio X Fretensis
  • Role in 70 AD: Camped on the Mount of Olives directly facing the Temple. It remained as the permanent garrison in Jerusalem after the war. Livius.org brewminate.com

Legio V Macedonica

  • Original Foundation: Raised in 43 BC.
  • Provincial Origin / Makeup: Stationed in the Balkans and Moesia (modern-day Bulgaria/Romania region) before the war, its soldiers were Danubian and Balkan provincials who spoke Greek or Latin.
  • Role in 70 AD: Approached Jerusalem from the west and camped near the main forces. Siege of Jerusalem Oxford Academic Legio X Fretensis

Legio XV Apollinaris

  • Original Foundation: Raised by Octavian in 41/40 BC.
  • Provincial Origin / Makeup: Stationed on the Danube frontier at Carnuntum (in modern-day Austria/Hungary), its legionaries were largely recruited from Pannonian provincials.
  • Role in 70 AD: Shared a large camp with the 12th legion on Mount Scopus to the northeast of Jerusalem. brill.com Roman Jewish Wars 4 Legio X Fretensis

Legio XII Fulminata

  • Original Foundation: Originally levied by Julius Caesar in 58 BC. Rome and Constantinople
  • Provincial Origin / Makeup: Long stationed in the East, its soldiers by 70 AD were composed almost entirely of Syrians and local eastern provincials. Legio X Fretensis Rome and Constantinople
  • Role in 70 AD: Participated in the early stages of the northern assault before being redeployed to the Euphrates frontier in Cappadocia.
